Salmon


Introduction

Salmon, the common name for various ray-finned fish species from the genera Salmo and Oncorhynchus, belongs to the Salmonidae family. Found in North Atlantic and North Pacific tributaries, they're closely related to trout, char, and other coldwater fish in subarctic and temperate regions, including Central Asia.

Description

Salmon, revered for its vibrant pink hue and robust flavor, reigns as a versatile fish native to the Northern Hemisphere. Whether enjoyed fresh, smoked, or canned, its oily texture and distinct taste elevate culinary experiences.

Nutrition

Salmon boasts a nutritional treasure chest, brimming with lean protein and omega-3 fatty acids that enrich heart health and combat inflammation. Enriched with essential vitamins like vitamin D and minerals like selenium, it nourishes the body, supporting vitality and overall health.

Basa Fish


Introduction

Basa, also known as Pangasius or Swai, emerges as a mild-flavored freshwater fish originating from Southeast Asia. Praised for its affordability and adaptability in various cuisines, it has earned a place on tables worldwide.

Description

Basa fish are usually sold when they reach over 8 ounces, with two batches annually and a typical market size of 0.8-1.0 kilograms per fish. Basa farming boasts low disease rates, often without needing extra oxygenation. Farming methods include flow-through systems or 20-30% daily water changes, with yields of 23-27 tons per hectare.

Nutrition

Basa offers a protein-rich foundation with fewer calories, enriching diets while aiding in weight management endeavors. Though lower in omega-3 fatty acids compared to salmon, its nutritional value is enriched with essential nutrients like vitamin D and selenium, promoting overall health.

Codfish


Introduction

Codfish, renowned for its mild, delicate taste and firm texture, thrives in the cold waters of the North Atlantic and Pacific oceans. Its versatility in culinary applications makes it a cherished ingredient in diverse cuisines.

Description

Gadus cods have three dorsal and two anal fins, small pelvic fins, and a distinct white lateral line. They possess an upper jaw with a chin barbel and medium-sized eyes. Atlantic cod can change color, with an average weight of 5–12 kilograms (11–26 pounds), occasionally reaching up to 100 kg (220 lb). Pacific cod are smaller and darker than their Atlantic counterparts.

Nutrition

Codfish presents a bountiful source of lean protein, enriched with essential nutrients that support weight management goals and overall health. Its nutritional profile is further enriched with vitamin B12, phosphorus, and selenium, contributing to a balanced diet that nurtures well-being.
